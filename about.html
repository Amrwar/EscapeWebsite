About

Built as an escape

A barbershop in Mivida for people who want the half hour to be worth something.

Most grooming is a queue and a countdown. Escape was built for the opposite — a room you don't mind waiting in, a chair that lets your shoulders drop, and a barber who asks what you want before anything is switched on.

The fit-out says it before anyone does: pale concrete walls, black steel, arched mirrors, a run of light across the ceiling, and enough space between chairs that you're never sitting in someone else's appointment. There's a sofa by the window and a pedicure area at the back, because a visit here isn't only about the top of your head.

The mark

Two climbers,
one wall

The figure in the logo is a climber, and inside the shop two of them scale the concrete above the sofa, lit from the track overhead. It's the clearest way to say what the name means: getting out of the day for a while, then coming back sharper.

The same idea runs through the rest of the fit-out — the stone reception, the arched mirrors, the low light at the back. Nothing shouts. It's a room you can sit in.

The standard

Detail is the whole job

Anyone can take length off. The difference is what the cut looks like a fortnight later.

  1. 01

    The consultation is part of the cut

    What shape you want, how you wear it during the week, and what your hair will actually hold — settled before the clippers come out.

  2. 02

    Clean tools, every client

    Blades sharpened rather than swapped out, guards cleaned between clients, fresh towels each time.

  3. 03

    A razor edge to finish

    The neck, the temple, the line at the ear. The last five minutes are what people notice for the next two weeks.

  4. 04

    Nobody is rushed out of the chair

    A cut takes half an hour because that's how long it takes. Treatments take as long as they take.

The shelf

What we put on your hair

Kérastase and L'Oréal on the shelf, not whatever was cheapest that month — for the oil baths, the protein treatments and the colour work as much as for what goes in at the end.

If something suits your hair, the barber will tell you which one and how much to use. If nothing does, they'll tell you that too.
